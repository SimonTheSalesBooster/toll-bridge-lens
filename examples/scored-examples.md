## Five lessons from scoring 115 stocks

**1. Real toll bridges are rare.** Of 115 quality names on our watchlist, 20 scored 12+. Most great companies are STRONG TOLL: good bridges with a visible competitor.

**2. The textbook bridges are boring.** Top scores: quarries (Martin Marietta, Vulcan: stone is too heavy to truck far and new permits are near impossible), ratings (Moody's: no bond gets issued without one), certified aircraft parts (TransDigm), index fees (MSCI), proxy distribution (Broadridge), semiconductor process control (KLA).

**3. The Gordie Howe test catches what earnings miss.** FICO: must-have score, huge toll hikes, near-zero cost. Then the US housing regulator admitted VantageScore for mortgages: a cheaper bridge right next door. Q3 drops to 0, BYPASS RISK, while earnings still looked fine.

| Company | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 | Q5 | Q6 | Q7 | Total | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FICO | 2 | 1 | 0 | 2 | 2 | 0 | 2 | 9 | STRONG TOLL · BYPASS RISK · GOV RISK |

**4. The government is often the real owner of the toll.** Apple's App Store (up to 30%) is a perfect bridge on paper, and courts and the EU are cutting it. Airports (Aena, ASUR) and utilities are monopolies whose toll a regulator sets.

**5. A great bridge can still keep the toll from you.** Arm collects a royalty on nearly every phone chip (a pure toll), but a 90% owner and no payouts mean the toll doesn't reach minority holders yet. Heavy stock pay does the same at Palantir and Shopify.

## And two anti-bridges that are still good businesses

Wise and HEICO win by charging LESS than the incumbent toll. They are the second bridge. Great businesses, different model: score them low on Q4 and judge them on volume instead.
