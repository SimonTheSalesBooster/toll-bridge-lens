# The Toll Bridge Model

Source: Michael Fritzell, "Toll bridge investing", Asian Century Stocks, 25 June 2026. https://www.asiancenturystocks.com/toll-bridge-investing/
Everything below is our own paraphrase and our own scoring. Read the original: it is short and good.

---

## 1. The idea in one line

The best businesses sit on a scarce, unavoidable chokepoint with a steady flow of traffic. Everyone who wants to get to the other side pays a toll. The bridge was paid for long ago, it costs almost nothing to run, and the owner can raise the toll over time.

Warren Buffett used a real bridge to explain it: the Ambassador Bridge between Detroit and Windsor. Per the article, Matty Moroun bought it in 1979 for about $30 million. The passenger toll went from $0.75 to $10 (roughly a tripling after inflation). It earns more than $60 million a year from about 6.5 million crossings, around a quarter of the trucks moving between the US and Canada. The capital was spent in "old dollars" and needs little replacement. That is the dream: pricing power plus almost no reinvestment.

## 2. The four parts of a toll bridge

| Part | Question | What strong looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Destination** | Why do people want to get to the other side? | They can say why in one sentence. It solves a functional problem, entertains, gives community, lowers risk (the trusted name), or signals status (Veblen goods) |
| **Traffic** | How much flows over the bridge, and is it growing? | Volume grows for structural reasons: a new technology, a shift in what people prefer, better infrastructure feeding the bridge |
| **Bypass risk** | Can people get there another way? | No practical alternative. Switching is painful or pointless. Nobody is building a second bridge |
| **Operating cost** | What does each crossing cost the owner? | Close to zero. A toll booth. High variable costs eat the value of the chokepoint |

## 3. What kills a toll bridge

1. **Traffic falls.** The destination gets less attractive: cheaper batteries hurt engine parts makers; a pandemic empties airports.
2. **Someone builds a second bridge.** The Gordie Howe International Bridge opens in 2026 next to the Ambassador Bridge and charges about half the toll. A bypass rarely looks dangerous until it opens.
3. **The government takes the toll.** Rate caps, recurring concession fees, forced access, break-ups. Anything regulated has a partner who can change the price.
4. **The owner keeps the toll.** A great bridge whose management refuses dividends or sensible buybacks is a value trap for minority shareholders.

## 4. A moat is not a return

The toll bridge tells you the business is protected. It does not tell you the stock will go up. A stock's return only comes from five places:

1. Higher revenue
2. Higher margins
3. Fewer shares (buybacks)
4. A higher valuation multiple
5. The dividend

A perfect bridge bought at a price that halves over five years can still lose money. That is why this lens always ends with the return sources (`tools/tollbridge.py returns`) and, for compounders, a reverse DCF (see the companion Mauboussin ROIC Lens).

## 5. The bridges the article walks through (paraphrased)

| Company | Destination | Traffic | Bypass | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Texas Pacific Land** | Access to oil and gas in the Permian | Horizontal drilling and fracking turned the Permian into a giant | Owns the surface land itself (hundreds of thousands of acres): drillers can't route around it | Close to zero for royalties and land rights |
| **Amadeus** | The global airline booking network | Jet engines and cheaper flights grew air travel for decades | In theory airlines could go direct; in practice network effects lock travel agents and airlines in | Each extra booking costs almost nothing |
| **Hong Kong Exchanges & Clearing** | Capital for Chinese companies, China access for foreign investors | China's equity market grew after the reform era | A licensed monopoly; Stock Connect access is scarce | Very high gross margins |

## 6. Toll-bridge-adjacent models

Not every bridge is made of concrete. The article lists models that behave like one:

- **Embedded enterprise software** (e.g. accounting software): the business runs on it, so switching is painful.
- **Testing and certification:** a product can't ship until it passes.
- **Razor and blade:** an installed base keeps buying consumables (e.g. sleep-apnea devices and masks).
- **Distribution bottlenecks:** a local monopoly on getting product onto shelves.
- **Data lock-in:** a credit bureau's accumulated borrower data.
- **Standards:** engineers trained on one platform (e.g. a GPU programming standard) don't want to learn another.

## 7. How to research one (from the article, plus our questions)

- **Talk to the people who pay the toll.** Would they switch? What would make them?
- **Find out who signs the check.** If the toll is a rounding error in their total cost, they won't fight it.
- **Ask how price-sensitive they are.** A toll that feels big invites a bypass.
- **Look for the second bridge.** Who is building a cheaper path, and when does it open?
- **Read the regulator.** Who can cap the toll, and are they talking about it?
- **Follow the cash.** Does the toll come back to you as dividends and buybacks?

## 8. Our scoring (7 questions, 0-2 each, max 14)

| # | Question | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | **Destination** | Nice-to-have | Wanted, substitutes exist | Must-have for the payer |
| Q2 | **Traffic** | Flat or shrinking | Growing with the economy | Structural tailwind |
| Q3 | **No bypass** | A real alternative exists or is being built | Competitors exist but switching hurts | No practical alternative |
| Q4 | **Toll power** | Price set by market or regulator | Some pricing power | Toll small vs payer's cost and rising faster than inflation |
| Q5 | **Cheap to run** | Heavy variable cost or heavy capex | Moderate | Near-zero cost per crossing, little capex |
| Q6 | **Government** | Toll capped / concession / active attack | Some regulatory or political risk | Little interference |
| Q7 | **Owners paid** | Owner keeps the toll (no returns, heavy dilution, controlling holder) | Some returns | Dividends and buybacks, aligned management |

**Verdict:** 12-14 TOLL BRIDGE · 9-11 STRONG TOLL · 6-8 CONTESTED · 0-5 NO TOLL.
**Red flags cap the verdict at STRONG TOLL:** Q3 = 0 (BYPASS RISK), Q6 = 0 (GOV RISK), Q7 = 0 with 9+ (VALUE TRAP RISK).

The thresholds and the flags are our choice, not the article's. The article gives the four parts, the four risks and the five return sources; turning them into a number is ours, so argue with it.
